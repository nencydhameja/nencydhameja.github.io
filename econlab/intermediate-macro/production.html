Production & Factor Prices

Cobb–Douglas production

\[Y = A\,K^{\alpha} L^{1-\alpha}\]

  • \(A\) = total factor productivity, \(K\) = capital, \(L\) = labor, \(\alpha\) = capital’s share.
  • Marginal products: \(MPL = (1-\alpha)\dfrac{Y}{L}\),   \(MPK = \alpha\dfrac{Y}{K}\).
  • Factor prices: real wage \(W/P = MPL\); real rental \(R/P = MPK\).
  • Income shares are constant: labor gets \((1-\alpha)Y\), capital gets \(\alpha Y\) — they add up to \(Y\) (Euler).

Try it. Add capital and watch the wage rise but the rental rate fall. Change \(\alpha\) to re-slice the pie between labor and capital.

What to notice

  • More capital → wage up, rental down (diminishing returns to capital).
  • More labor → wage down, rental up.
  • The shares (\(1-\alpha\) labor, \(\alpha\) capital) never change with K or L — only moving \(\alpha\) re-slices the pie.
